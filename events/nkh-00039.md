    text: "On 16 October 2024, the North Korean hacker group Citrine Sleet exploited a series of vulnerabilities in the smart contracts of the Radiant Capital loan protocol, manipulating smart contracts on two blockchains, BNB Smart Chain and Arbitrum, and gaining access to three private keys of signatories to a multi-signing wallet, which controlled the functions of the protocol, resulting in the theft of about $51.5 million from protocol users. The attack was initially reported by De.Fi, another security company, who warned users of the attack through a post on X, clarifying that changes to smart contracts had taken place fraudulently. In response to the attack, security company Ancilia inadvertently posted a link to a wallet drainer, while trying to assist victims, which was promptly removed after the community noticed the error. The incident was widely reported and commented on by security experts, who highlighted the importance of clear and transparent information by security companies, and the need for a strict verification protocol and responsible communications. Radiant Capital claimed to be aware of the problem and took steps to cooperate with several security experts, including Mandiant, SEAL911, Hyperactive, ZeroShadow and Chainalysis, to investigate and resolve the crisis. On 6th December, the Radiant Capital released a summary of the technical report by Mandiant on Medium including an attribution of the attack to the North Korean hacker group Citrine Sleet."
